**1. Substantive findings, ranked by seriousness**

1. **The financing presentation is described beyond the expressly permitted facts.**  
   Exact text:
   > “A financing presentation of this date sets out proposed metabolic-health and therapeutics expansion themes. Those themes depend on board approval, regulatory requirements, investor suitability, and final terms.”

   Also:
   > “Financing presentation outlining proposed expansion themes.”

   Only the presentation’s existence and date are verified. Its themes and conditions are not permitted disclosures. “Final terms” also suggests a transaction process beyond a general growth objective. The subsequent disclaimer does not cure this.

   **Correction:** “A financing presentation is dated August 25, 2026.” Use this wording consistently in both places.

2. **The development descriptions invent substantive program scope.**  
   Exact text:
   > “Pharmacist-selected non-drug supplies, onboarding and refill support, and fulfilment under an agreed scope. Medication is excluded.”

   > “Starter equipment, usage-driven consumables, clinic procurement, kitting and fulfilment, and contracted non-clinical workflow support.”

   The verified facts establish two programs in development, with pharmacist selection and medication exclusion for the pharmacy program. They do not establish onboarding, refill support, kitting, contracted workflow support or the other detailed components. Calling these proposals does not make them verified.

   **Corrections:**  
   Pharmacy: “Non-drug supply programs for pharmacies are in development. Supplies would be pharmacist-selected; medication is excluded.”  
   Metabolic health: “Metabolic-health supply services are in development and are not available today.”

   The contact wording “Non-drug supplies and fulfilment for a pharmacy” should likewise lose the unverified fulfilment component.

3. **The business case asserts unverified existing operations and relationships.**  
   Exact text:
   > “The group's online medical-supply commerce, its fulfilment arrangements, and the clinic supply work already running.”

   > “The same commerce and fulfilment base, plus clinic equipment sourcing and the procurement relationships that come with clinic work.”

   Existing fulfilment arrangements, ongoing clinic supply work and procurement relationships are not among the verified facts. These claims give the proposed programs an unsupported operational foundation.

   **Correction:** “The group operates online supply businesses in Canada and the United States and provides clinic planning, design, construction, fit-out and equipment services for British Columbia projects.”

4. **The copy invents development gates and a future publication commitment.**  
   Exact text:
   > “Product selection, fulfilment responsibilities, service scope, and commercial terms have to be agreed in writing with each partner. Terms are not established, so no revenue mechanism is stated.”

   > “Pathway contents, device compatibility, fulfilment capacity, and service scope have to be confirmed per program. Availability is published only as each pathway is confirmed.”

   These are unverified requirements and process claims. “No revenue mechanism is stated” is internal editorial language, while the availability sentence promises a publication process and implies eventual availability.

   **Correction:** Remove these conditions fields. Retain one disclosure: “Both programs are in development and are not available today.”

5. **The clinic copy implies verified delivery arrangements and a specific quotation service without evidence.**  
   Exact text:
   > “within verified delivery arrangements.”

   This appears twice in the clinic content. Also:
   > “Room-by-room equipment planning and a written quote for a new or renovated clinic.”

   The facts establish clinic services for British Columbia projects, but no delivery arrangements or room-by-room written quotation service. “Verified” also exposes source-validation language to readers.

   **Corrections:**  
   Clinic description: “Planning, design, construction, fit-out and equipment for clinic projects in British Columbia.”  
   Contact route: “Equipment inquiries for clinic projects in British Columbia.”

6. **The acquisition route promises confidentiality that has not been established.**  
   Exact text:
   > “A first confidential conversation about an acquisition or a strategic arrangement.”

   A verified email address does not establish a confidentiality undertaking. The inquiry invitation itself does not imply an active transaction.

   **Correction:** “Inquiries about potential acquisitions or strategic arrangements.”

**2. Weaker findings**

- “In development; nothing is sold from this site” describes the website’s sales mechanism rather than clearly stating program availability. Prefer “In development; not available today.”
- “The stated direction,” “strategic counterparties” and “the dated public record” sound bureaucratic. Prefer “The group’s objective,” “potential strategic partners” and “dated company information.”
- “Which do you need?” and “A clinic project, or supplies for a clinic already open” depart from the requested third-person voice. “Clinic projects and supplies for existing clinics” is plainer.
- Development status is repeated in the homepage description, qualification and investor development content. Some repetition across pages is useful; repeated caveats within one passage can be consolidated.
- “Each one reaches … the page that answers it” overpromises what a routing page can answer. Prefer “Inquiry routes lead to the relevant business page or LifeSupply email address.”

- Clinic projects and supply purchasing are presented as separate routes.
- Development status is more explicit, and the public promise of a future form has been removed.
