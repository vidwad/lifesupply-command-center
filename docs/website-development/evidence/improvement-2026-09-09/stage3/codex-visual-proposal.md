Recommend **two visuals, both replacing existing presentations rather than adding sections**. Build the Clinic Solutions choice first: it resolves the most consequential ambiguity—whether a visitor needs a clinic project or simply wants to order supplies.

Use the existing square edges, condensed uppercase headings and red top rules. Keep explanatory copy in readable body type. Red should provide emphasis, not encode business status on its own.

### 1. Clinic project or routine supplies?

**Visitor question:** “Do I need help opening or renovating a clinic, or supplies for one already operating?”

**Page and position:** Clinic Solutions, immediately after the introductory copy and before either service’s detailed content. Replace the current presentation of the two entries with this choice.

**Form:** Reuse the **numbered icon feature grid**, reduced to two editorial cards. Omit numbering and directional arrows: these are independent choices, not successive steps. A new diagram or photograph would add little.

**Desktop:** Two equally prominent cards:

- **Opening or renovating a clinic** — British Columbia projects; planning, design, construction, fit-out and equipment. Link directly to project information.
- **Supplying an open clinic** — routine procurement through the operating stores; no project required. Link directly to the store directory.

Use descriptive link labels, such as “Explore clinic projects” and “Find a supply store.”

**Phone:** Stack the complete cards at 390 px, project first and routine supplies second. Keep each explanation and link visible without expanding anything.

**Text equivalent:** The cards themselves are semantic HTML: “For opening or renovating a clinic in British Columbia, explore planning, design, construction, fit-out and equipment services. For routine procurement for an operating clinic, visit the operating stores; no clinic project is required.”

**Must not imply:** That procurement requires a project engagement, that project customers automatically become store customers, or that the two choices share an account or contract.

**Verdict:** **Build first.** Clear visual separation directly supports the visitor’s next action. It earns its place by replacing potentially ambiguous service navigation.

### 2. Four websites, four clear destinations

**Visitor question:** “Which website serves my location and need, and what should I expect when I go there?”

**Page and position:** Medical Supplies, immediately after its introduction, as the primary four-website directory. Replace the laptop-frame storefront presentation wherever it currently performs that role.

**Form:** Reuse the **bento grid**, with four equally weighted HTML cards. Current website names and explicit destination facts are more useful here than dated screenshots. No connectors, shared hub or enclosing “platform” diagram.

**Desktop:** A two-by-two directory with the same field order in every card: website, customer or purpose, geography, currency where applicable, and destination link.

| Website | Information to make immediately visible |
|---|---|
| lifesupply.ca | Medical supplies · Canada · CAD |
| wellmartmedical.com | Medical supplies · Canada · CAD |
| balkowitsch.com | Medical supplies · United States · USD |
| www.lifesupplyclinics.com | Clinic project services · British Columbia · currency not specified in the supplied brief |

Use verified customer descriptions from existing site copy to distinguish the stores further; the brief does not establish those distinctions. Label the fourth destination explicitly as **project services**.

Place a visible sentence above the cards: “Each website has separate accounts and support. Check the currency shown for your destination.”

**Phone:** Stack cards in the same order. Repeat field labels within each card so visitors never need to remember a column heading. Put the destination link after its facts.

**Text equivalent:** The HTML directory is its own text equivalent. Each card reads as a complete destination description, followed by a clearly named external link.

**Must not imply:** A shared login, cart, checkout, support service, customer record or customer relationship. Geographic and currency labels must not be presented as promises about shipping coverage. Do not invent a currency for the services website.

**Verdict:** **Build.** A consistent comparison makes materially different destinations easier to distinguish. Its value comes from explicit facts, not decorative graphics.
